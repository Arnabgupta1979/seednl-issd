# E02 + G01 pilot: mapping, storyboard, choices and debrief

Status: pilot for review. Everything here is proposed. Nothing is approved course content.

## 1. Fit with the V2.0 concept note

Source: *ISSD Course Concept Note, SeedNL, V2.0, 06 Nov 2026*. Only the wording of the note is used for the mapping. No budget or other Downloads files were used.

| V2.0 element | How E02 + G01 connect to it |
|---|---|
| **Module 1, "Why an integrated approach matters"** (primary home) | The module asks participants to "trace how farmers obtain seed of two crops in one setting" and to identify "whose experience is missing from the initial account". E02 introduces the idea. G01 lets participants trace seed routes for three fictional farmers and three crops (maize, tomato, beans) and ends in a "whose voice is missing?" prompt. |
| **Objective 1**: "Diagnose a seed system using ISSD and explain how sector functions interact" | Main objective. The debrief maps each seed route to the eight sector functions and shows which functions a farmer's choice depends on. This is a first, simplified step toward diagnosis. It does not replace Module 2. |
| **Objective 2**: seed production, distribution and community seed banking affect agrobiodiversity and farmers' access to diverse crops and varieties | Secondary. The Neema (beans) profile includes a community seed bank. Saved seed, exchange and community routes keep varietal diversity in view. The pilot does not assess climate adaptation. |
| **Module 2**, "Diagnose a seed sector" | Bridge only. The debrief uses the eight functions as a lens and leaves the diagnosis to Module 2. |
| **Module 4**, "Seed markets, policy and access to finance" | Light touchpoint. Cash timing and credit appear as a constraint (financing). Regulation appears as "what quality assurance does this route offer?". No policy content is taught. |
| **Module 5**, "Partnerships and contested choices" | Light touchpoint. The debrief asks who decides, who carries the risk and whose voice is missing. |
| Objectives 3–5, Module 6 | Not addressed. |

The eight sector functions used (V2.0 order): production; value addition and distribution; service provision; utilisation; stakeholder organisation; regulation; coordination; financing. They are **not** the eight GSST 2022 course sections.

- Evidence review of the generic seed-route statements (see the source register in the game).
- Local adaptation: the farmers, places and numbers here are fictional.

## 2. G01 design stance

- No source of seed is "best". For each farmer, every route meets some needs and misses others. Different farmers get different answers.
- Five routes: farmer-saved seed; neighbours and kin (social exchange); local market or grain trader; cooperative or community seed producer; commercial seed company or agro-dealer.
- No points, scores or probabilities. Feedback is qualitative: a "needs and constraints" grid with words and symbols, uncertainty stated, and a stress test.
- All farmer facts are fictional and synthetic. They are labelled as such. Generic statements are hedged ("often", "may") and listed in a source register as needing expert verification.

## 4. G01 storyboard and choices

1. **Pick a farmer** (replay with a different farmer for comparison).
2. **Meet the farmer.** Explicit card: crop, what they want from seed (preferences), what they have (cash, labour, time, travel, storage, information), and the seasonal calendar with the seed decision window and constraints.
3. **Explore routes.** Open any of five routes to see what it can offer this farmer and what to check.
4. **Plan.** Choose one main route and, optionally, one back-up route. A mix is allowed and realistic.
5. **Stress test.** Pick one disruption (late rains, cash shortfall, poor seed batch, variety not available). The game shows how the plan holds and what the farmer would do next, in words.
6. **Fit report.** Needs × routes grid for the chosen routes, uncertainty ("what we do not know"), and what to check before committing.
7. **Debrief.** The full fit map for all five routes, the eight-function lens, reflection prompts, and a compare view across farmers you have tried.

Facilitator note, source register and rubric are in the game and in `G01_Farmer_seed_choices/docs`.

## 5. Debrief questions (also shown in the game)

1. Which need drove your first choice? Did a different farmer change your answer?
2. Which route did you rank last? Under what circumstances might it be the best?
3. Which sector functions did your plan depend on? Which were weak or invisible?
4. Who is missing from this story (for example women who keep seed, young farmers, seed sellers, extension staff)? What would they tell you?
5. If you were a programme manager, would you strengthen a route, connect two routes, or leave a route alone? Why?
